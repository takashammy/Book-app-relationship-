# Design directions: relationship & intimacy editorial reader

A ranked brief for a **top-tier editorial reading sanctuary** about relationships and intimacy — daily reader, library, calendar, optional English/Chinese. This is not a dating product and not a summary mill.

We are **leaving** Luminary’s Swiss-poster / warm-paper system (newsprint `#FAF6EE`, charcoal frames, 4-column registration grid, rotated vertical spines, Newsreader + Inter badges). That language reads like a philosophy broadsheet. Intimacy needs a room you enter, not a poster you decode.

Source vocabulary: [Himanshu Bhardwaj, “50 Design Styles Every Designer Should Know for Better Prompting”](https://uxplanet.org/50-design-styles-every-designer-should-know-for-better-prompting-56c09d55db62) (UX Planet). Mixes below use those named styles only.

Later rounds: [clay-sanctuary-variations.md](./clay-sanctuary-variations.md) (parked) · [new-mixes.md](./new-mixes.md) (five unused mixes).

**Why this order.** Velvet Reading Room is the strongest default: literary, private, bilingual-capable, and farthest from both swipe culture and digest cards. Clay Sanctuary is the best East–West craft language if the product leans bilingual from day one. Botanical Letter is the most sensual-without-dating voice, but ornament can crowd a daily reader. Candle Study is the clearest after-hours identity. Dusk Glass is the most contemporary “app,” and the easiest to dilute into generic wellness.

## 1. Velvet Reading Room

**Mix:** Ethereal × Luxury Typography × Light Academia

**Source styles:** *Ethereal* (weightless pastels, gauzy overlays, low contrast); *Luxury Typography* (refined serifs, wide tracking, type as identity); *Light Academia* (creamy neutrals, linen, sunlight, scholarly calm).

**Why it fits.** A relationship sanctuary should feel like a private library at late morning — not a match queue, not a productivity stack. This mix treats intimacy as literature: slow, well-set, slightly hushed. English display type can carry the brand; Chinese Song/Ming sits beside it as equal scholarly text, not a subtitle. Daily read, library, and calendar all read as *volumes and days*, not streaks.

**Palette / type / layout**
- Palette: unbleached ivory `#F7F1E8`, linen `#E8DFD2`, pale blush `#E8D4CE`, dried sage `#A8B5A3`, warm stone ink `#3F3A36`. Contrast stays soft; no charcoal frames.
- Type: high-craft serif for titles and pull quotes (Newsreader is too “poster”; prefer something closer to *Freight Text*, *Ivar*, or *Songti / Noto Serif CJK*). Metadata in a quiet humanist sans, sentence case, not tracked Inter badges.
- Layout: one generous text column, huge margins, a single pull-quote as the only “module.” Library as a shelf of titled spines or quiet cards, not a filter dashboard. Calendar as a linen month, not a habit heatmap.

**Avoid.** Swiss hairlines and registration marks; vertical rotated spines; gold-foil luxury-fashion cliché; hearts, ribbons, or Coquette pink; Blinkist-style numbered “key takeaways” chips; couple photography as chrome.

**Still:** `media/design-direction-01.png` — tablet daily-reader moment, cream field, bilingual title, one quote.

## 2. Clay Sanctuary

**Mix:** Japandi × Wabi Sabi

**Source styles:** *Japandi* (Japanese quiet + Scandinavian use; light woods, beige/gray, clean sans, natural materials); *Wabi Sabi* (imperfection, earth, asymmetry, contemplative pause).

**Why it fits.** Intimacy is practice, not performance. This mix is the only direction that *natively* holds English and Chinese without looking like a translation overlay: ink, clay, empty space. It frames the product as a tea room for two (or for one, honestly), not a magazine cover and not a dating lounge. Calendar and behavior prompts feel like tending, not tracking.

**Palette / type / layout**
- Palette: raw oat `#E6DCCF`, kiln clay `#C4A484`, ash `#8C8680`, ink-wash `#2C2A28` used sparingly, celadon `#7F9C8E`, unglazed white.
- Type: humanist sans for UI (something like *Source Han Sans / Source Sans*); one restrained serif or kaishu-adjacent face for quotes only. No monograms, no display drama.
- Layout: asymmetric calm — a thick empty margin, one ceramic-colored panel, titles set low. Library as a few large, imperfect tiles. Calendar as a sparse grid with a single marked day.

**Avoid.** Swiss 4-column posters; gold luxury; spa-stock bamboo and stacked stones; dating-app rose; Bento dashboards; Neo-Brutalist stickers; perfectly even “wellness” mint.

**Still:** `media/design-direction-02.png` — phone library, clay tiles, bilingual titles, empty air.

Five more rooms in this mix: [clay-sanctuary-variations.md](./clay-sanctuary-variations.md).

## 3. Botanical Letter

**Mix:** Art Nouveau × Filigree

**Source styles:** *Art Nouveau* (fluid botanicals, hand-lettered type, earthy stained-glass color); *Filigree* (fine metallic line, ceremonial editorial ornament — used as *hairline* decoration, not jewelry).

**Why it fits.** Desire and care already have a visual tradition that is not Tinder and not IKEA: vines, letters, the body as plant. This mix makes the daily read feel like a correspondence — a letter you open — which is the right metaphor for intimacy writing. Sensual without flesh; decorative without wedding-invitation kitsch if the ornament stays in the margins.

**Palette / type / layout**
- Palette: moss `#5C6B4E`, dusty rose `#C08984`, parchment `#F3E6D0`, oxidized copper `#8A6A4F`, muted line-gold `#C2A36A` at 1px only.
- Type: an organic display serif for the English title (think *Art Nouveau* poster lettering, simplified); Chinese in a calligraphic Song or regular script, never a novelty “oriental” face. Body in a readable old-style serif.
- Layout: a living frame — vine or whiplash line at the edge of the column, never a Swiss box. Library cards as pressed-plant plates. Calendar as a botanical almanac (month name large, days small).

**Avoid.** Baroque / Victorian pile-up; lace, pearls, bows (that is Coquette / Shabby Chic); full stained-glass wallpaper behind body text; gold-leaf luxury packaging; erotic illustration; Memphis squiggles.

**Still:** `media/design-direction-03.png` — phone reader, vine frame, moss/rose, letter-like essay.

## 4. Candle Study

**Mix:** Tenebrism × Dark Magic Academia (scholarly cut) × Luxury Typography

**Source styles:** *Tenebrism* (chiaroscuro, black field, one spotlight); *Dark Magic Academia* (antique study, black/gold, serif scholarship — **without** spellbooks, witches, or Harry Potter props); *Luxury Typography* (type as the only ornament).

**Why it fits.** Most intimacy reading happens at night, in private. This is the after-hours sanctuary: a single lit page in a dark room. Emotional weight without gothic costume. It also solves bilingual hierarchy cleanly — English and Chinese can share the same candlelit column, one size, no “secondary language” tint. Distinct from every pale direction above, so it is the natural dark mode *and* a viable primary identity.

**Palette / type / layout**
- Palette: umber black `#16110E`, candle cream `#E8C99A`, oxidised wine `#6E2A32` (not dating crimson), antique brass `#A3844F` as hairline only, warm gray type `#D8CDBE`.
- Type: high-contrast modern or old-style serif, large; small caps for section labels (`TODAY`, `今日`). No blackletter. No Inter badges.
- Layout: one spotlighted column on a void. Quote in a pool of light. Library as a dark shelf with titles catching the lamp. Calendar as a dim month with today as the only ember.

**Avoid.** Neon Cybercore / Synthwave; full Gothic blackletter and stained-glass horror; occult kitsch (candles-as-clipart, moons, tarot); swipe cards; crimson heart CTAs; high-key paper from Luminary.

**Still:** `media/design-direction-04.png` — phone night reader, chiaroscuro, one illuminated essay.

## 5. Dusk Glass

**Mix:** Aurora × Glassmorphism × Ethereal

**Source styles:** *Aurora* (iridescent gradients, soft glow, meditative motion); *Glassmorphism* (frosted panels, blur, layered depth); *Ethereal* (air, low contrast, romance without sugar).

**Why it fits.** If the product must feel like a *contemporary* app — not a print facsimile — this is the honest modern option. Twilight is the right metaphor: the hour between two people. Frosted panels keep the reader, library, and calendar as separate rooms in one dusk. Risk is highest here (wellness / fintech glass). The brief only holds if type stays literary and the aurora stays dusk-colored, not aqua-bubble.

**Palette / type / layout**
- Palette: dusk violet `#3D3558`, pearl `#F4F0EA`, champagne glow `#E6CBA8`, soft teal `#7FA8A4`, translucent white at 20–35% over a slow gradient. No chrome, no neon.
- Type: thin grotesque for chrome; a light serif for the essay. Chinese in a light Song, same size as English.
- Layout: 1–2 frosted plates over an aurora field. Reader is a glass page, not a card stack. Library as overlapping translucent volumes. Calendar as a frosted month floating on dusk.

**Avoid.** Y2K chrome and Neo Frutiger Aero bubbles; Cybercore grids; productivity Bento; dating-app gradients (hot pink → orange); glass-on-everything OS clones; Luminary paper and poster frames.

**Still:** `media/design-direction-05.png` — phone dusk home/reader, frosted essay plate on aurora.

## What none of these are

- **Not Luminary.** No newsprint poster, no 4-column bleed, no rotated `DURATION` spines, no Inter uppercase badges.
- **Not a dating app.** No profiles, no photos of couples as the brand, no hearts-as-navigation, no Coquette / Kitsch flirt.
- **Not Blinkist.** No stacked digest cards, no gamified streaks as the hero, no “finish in 6 min” as the visual identity (duration can exist as quiet metadata).

If we pick one to build against later: **Velvet Reading Room** as the light system, with **Candle Study** as its true dark mode — same type, inverted light. **Clay Sanctuary** is the alternate if bilingual craft is the product thesis.
