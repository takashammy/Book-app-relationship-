# Relationship & intimacy canon — 250 titles

An editorial inventory for **Keep** (閱室): a reading sanctuary focused only on relationship and intimacy — romantic life, attachment, communication, sex, marriage, family-of-origin as it scripts pairing, and friendship when friendship *is* the intimacy text. Modeled on Luminary (閱心): bilingual-capable, East–West, contemplative rather than a digest mill. Closest existing Luminary title: ***The Courage to Be Disliked***.

**This is not Luminary’s 15-title catalog.** Luminary mixes Camus, habits, money, sleep, and Stoicism. Those titles are out of scope here. The only shared book is *The Courage to Be Disliked*; everything else in this file is about relating.

This is a **shipping list**, not a syllabus and not a ranking of human worth. **1–30** are the must-read spine a product person can open the library with, ranked. **31–250** are inventory numbers inside categories, not a claim that 47 is “better” than 180. Books are **independent ideas** — no series volume numbers in the product.

## How this was chosen

- **On-topic only.** Classics, contemporary science, clinical/therapy, cultural and Eastern texts, and widely respected popular books. Generic self-help (habits, hustle, “be more confident”) is out unless the book is actually about relating.
- **Well-attested.** Titles a working clinician, scholar, or serious reader would recognize. No invented obscurities. A few mass-market books (love languages, Mars/Venus) are here because they are culturally inescapable, and the note says so.
- **Deduplicated.** One entry per work. Later editions, workbooks, and alternate translations are not extra titles. *He Comes Next* / *Passionista* is one book and is omitted in favor of Kerner’s more distinct later title. *True Love* (Hanh) is treated as overlapping *Teachings on Love* and is not listed separately.
- **Years.** First widely cited publication, with original-language year when that is the culturally primary edition. Classical works use scholarly circa. A year is a best estimate, not a first-printing affidavit.
- **Eastern resonance.** Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Buddhist, and Taoist *intimacy* titles sit beside the Western clinic. General Confucian or “life philosophy” classics are out unless the book is actually about pairing.
- **Chinese titles.** Listed only when a standard 繁/简 publishing title exists. 简 and 繁 are both given when the published names differ; omitted when there is no attested edition. No ISBNs (none invented).
- **Harm literacy.** Bancroft, Evans, Herman, and related titles are in **Harm, Power & Boundaries** so the product does not treat control as a “conflict style.”

## Must-read 30

Read these before any long tail. They are all relationship/intimacy titles — science, clinic, philosophy of love, sex, harm literacy, and East–West pairing — not Luminary’s general non-fiction shelf. *The Courage to Be Disliked* is first because it is the one Luminary title that already belongs in this category.

**1. *The Courage to Be Disliked*** — Ichiro Kishimi & Fumitake Koga (2013 JP / 2018 EN)
中文：简《被讨厌的勇气》／繁《被討厭的勇氣》
`Self in Relation` · Adlerian inner independence as the precondition for intimacy rather than fusion or approval-seeking — the one Luminary title that already belongs here.


**2. *The Art of Loving*** — Erich Fromm (1956)
中文：《爱的艺术》／《愛的藝術》
`Philosophy & History of Love` · The modern philosophical baseline: love as a practiced art of care, responsibility, respect, and knowledge — not a lucky feeling.


**3. *Attached*** — Amir Levine & Rachel S. F. Heller (2010)
中文：简《读懂恋人心》
`Attachment & Bonding` · The most widely used popular map of anxious, avoidant, and secure styles in adult romantic pairing.


**4. *Hold Me Tight*** — Sue Johnson (2008)
中文：简《爱的7种对话》／繁《抱緊我》
`Attachment & Bonding` · The public face of Emotionally Focused Therapy: seven conversations that turn protest and withdrawal into a secure bond.


**5. *The Seven Principles for Making Marriage Work*** — John M. Gottman & Nan Silver (1999)
中文：《幸福的婚姻》
`Marriage & Partnership` · The most cited observational-science guide to what actually predicts lasting partnership — and the Four Horsemen that sink it.


**6. *Mating in Captivity*** — Esther Perel (2006)
中文：简《亲密陷阱》／繁《情欲徒刑》
`Sex & Desire` · The essential paradox of long-term desire: security and erotic vitality pull in opposite directions.


**7. *Come As You Are*** — Emily Nagoski (2015)
中文：繁《性愛好科學》
`Sex & Desire` · The contemporary science of women’s desire — accelerators, brakes, context — without shame or a single “normal” body.


**8. *Nonviolent Communication*** — Marshall B. Rosenberg (1999)
中文：《非暴力沟通》／《非暴力溝通》
`Communication & Conflict` · The four-step language (observation, feeling, need, request) most therapists still hand couples who only know how to blame.


**9. *All About Love*** — bell hooks (2000)
中文：简《关于爱的一切》
`Philosophy & History of Love` · A definition of love as action and ethic, written against the culture that confuses romance, domination, and care.


**10. *Getting the Love You Want*** — Harville Hendrix (1988)
`Marriage & Partnership` · Imago therapy’s durable claim: we recreate childhood wounds in adult pairing, and can finish them together.


**11. *Teachings on Love*** — Thich Nhat Hanh (1997)
中文：《你可以，爱》／《你可以，愛》

**12. *The Dance of Anger*** — Harriet Lerner (1985)
中文：简《愤怒之舞》
`Communication & Conflict` · Still the clearest feminist-clinical book on how anger is information in intimate systems, especially for women taught to swallow it.


**13. *A General Theory of Love*** — Thomas Lewis, Fari Amini & Richard Lannon (2000)
中文：简《爱的起源》
`Attachment & Bonding` · A literary neuroscience of limbic regulation: we co-regulate the people we love, for better or worse.


**14. *Passionate Marriage*** — David Schnarch (1997)
`Sex & Desire` · Differentiation, not fusion, as the engine of erotic and emotional adulthood inside a committed pair.


**15. *Adult Children of Emotionally Immature Parents*** — Lindsay C. Gibson (2015)
中文：简《不成熟的父母》／繁《假性孤儿》
`Family of Origin` · The most useful recent map of how emotionally unavailable parents script adult pairing, guilt, and self-erasure.


**16. *The Mastery of Love*** — Don Miguel Ruiz (1999)
中文：《爱的修行》／《愛的修行》

## Attachment & Bonding (31–54)

Bowlby to the present: research, EFT/PACT, popular style books, and the science of pair-bonding. Skip quiz-only social posts; these are the volumes.

**31. *A Secure Base*** — John Bowlby (1988)
中文：《安全基地》
`Attachment & Bonding` · Bowlby’s later clinical essays: the caregiver as a secure base from which exploration — and later adult love — becomes possible.


**32. *Attachment*** — John Bowlby (1969)
中文：《依恋》／《依戀》
`Attachment & Bonding` · The founding monograph of attachment theory; later popular books are footnotes to this one. Listed as its own work, not as a series number.


**33. *The Making and Breaking of Affectional Bonds*** — John Bowlby (1979)
`Attachment & Bonding` · Shorter Bowlby on how bonds form and rupture — more usable than the full trilogy for an editorial reader.


**34. *Patterns of Attachment*** — Mary D. S. Ainsworth, Mary C. Blehar, Everett Waters & Sally Wall (1978)
`Attachment & Bonding` · The Strange Situation studies that gave the field secure, avoidant, and ambivalent as observable patterns.


**35. *Becoming Attached*** — Robert Karen (1994)
`Attachment & Bonding` · The best narrative history of Bowlby, Ainsworth, and the scientific fights that produced adult-attachment culture.


**36. *Attachment in Psychotherapy*** — David J. Wallin (2007)
中文：简《心理治疗中的依恋》
`Attachment & Bonding` · The clinician’s bridge from infant research to how adult patients (and lovers) use a mind to hold another mind.


**37. *Attachment Theory in Practice*** — Sue Johnson (2019)
`Attachment & Bonding` · Johnson’s later synthesis: EFT as a transdiagnostic attachment model, not only a couples-technique book.


**38. *The Science of Trust*** — John Gottman (2011)
`Attachment & Bonding` · Gottman’s research book on attunement, trust metrics, and betrayal inside the couple — not a general-psychology title.


**39. *The Practice of Emotionally Focused Couple Therapy*** — Sue Johnson (1996 / 2004)
`Attachment & Bonding` · The clinical manual (first titled Creating Connection): how EFT is actually done, session by session.


**40. *Polysecure*** — Jessica Fern (2020)
`Attachment & Bonding` · Attachment security rebuilt for consensual non-monogamy — and useful to monogamous readers who treat security as a practice.


**41. *Polywise*** — Jessica Fern (2023)
`Attachment & Bonding` · The follow-on: how people actually metabolize jealousy, hierarchy, and identity shock when a relationship structure opens.


**42. *Insecure in Love*** — Leslie Becker-Phelps (2014)
`Attachment & Bonding` · A clinical self-help book aimed at anxious pairing: compassion for the protest system without making it a personality sentence.


**43. *Anxiously Attached*** — Jessica Baum (2022)
`Attachment & Bonding` · A current somatic-leaning guide for the anxious-preoccupied pattern in dating and partnership.


**44. *Secure Love*** — Julie Menanno (2024)
`Attachment & Bonding` · A therapist-popular EFT translation for couples who already speak “attachment style” and need the cycle, not the quiz.


**45. *The Power of Attachment*** — Diane Poole Heller (2019)
`Attachment & Bonding` · Somatic/DARe practices for moving the attachment system in the body, not only in insight.


**46. *In Each Other's Care*** — Stan Tatkin (2023)
`Attachment & Bonding` · Tatkin’s later repair manual: daily practices for staying a team when threat physiology takes the wheel.


**47. *Attachment Disturbances in Adults*** — Daniel P. Brown & David S. Elliott (2016)
`Attachment & Bonding` · The heavy clinical text on treating severe attachment disturbance; the opposite of a quiz-book.


**48. *Adult Attachment*** — Mario Mikulincer & Phillip R. Shaver (2007)
`Attachment & Bonding` · The major research monograph on adult attachment processes in couple life, affect regulation, and caregiving.


**49. *Born for Love*** — Bruce D. Perry & Maia Szalavitz (2010)
`Attachment & Bonding` · Developmental trauma and empathy: why the capacity to love is grown in early relationship, not installed later as a tip.


**50. *Anxious in Love*** — Carolyn Daitch & Lissah Lorberbaum (2012)
`Attachment & Bonding` · How anxiety hijacks a partnership, with clinical tools written for the pair rather than for general mindfulness.


**51. *Wired to Connect*** — Amy Banks with Leigh Ann Hirschman (2015)
`Attachment & Bonding` · Relational-cultural theory plus neuroscience: we are built for mutuality, and isolation is a physiological injury.


**52. *Love 2.0*** — Barbara L. Fredrickson (2013)
`Attachment & Bonding` · A positive-psychology account of love as micro-moments of positivity resonance, not only a lifelong bond.


**53. *The Unexpected Legacy of Divorce*** — Judith S. Wallerstein, Julia M. Lewis & Sandra Blakeslee (2000)
`Attachment & Bonding` · A longitudinal look at how a parents’ divorce scripts the next generation’s pairing, trust, and fear of abandonment.


**54. *Loving with the Brain in Mind*** — Mona DeKoven Fishbane (2013)
`Attachment & Bonding` · Interpersonal neurobiology written for couples and couple therapists: the pair as a two-brain system.

## Philosophy & History of Love (235–250)

Philosophy, sociology, and history of love. Friendship is here only when the book treats it as a primary intimate bond — not as networking, and not as a general ethics or loneliness-policy title.

**235. *Platonic*** — Marisa G. Franco (2022)
`Philosophy & History of Love` · The rare science-backed book that treats friendship as an attachment relationship, not a leftover after romance.


**236. *Symposium*** — Plato (c. 385–370 BCE)
中文：《会饮篇》／《會飲篇》
`Philosophy & History of Love` · The Western philosophical origin story of eros, from bodily desire to the ladder of beauty; every later love theory answers it.


**237. *Eros the Bittersweet*** — Anne Carson (1986)
`Philosophy & History of Love` · A poet-classicist’s essay on Greek eros as lack and edge; the literary-philosophical counterweight to technique books.


**238. *On Love (De l'amour)*** — Stendhal (1822)
中文：《论爱情》／《論愛情》
`Philosophy & History of Love` · Crystallization and the psychology of falling in love — a book about romantic love, not a general spiritual almanac.


**239. *A Lover's Discourse*** — Roland Barthes (1977)
中文：《恋人絮语》／《戀人絮語》
`Philosophy & History of Love` · Fragments of the lover’s speech: waiting, jealousy, “I love you” as a scene, not a self-help program.


**240. *The Way We Never Were*** — Stephanie Coontz (1992)
`Philosophy & History of Love` · Debunks the 1950s family nostalgia that still silently judges contemporary partnerships.


**241. *The Transformation of Intimacy*** — Anthony Giddens (1992)
中文：简《亲密关系的变革》
`Philosophy & History of Love` · The sociological thesis of the “pure relationship” and confluent love: intimacy as democracy between equals.


**242. *Liquid Love*** — Zygmunt Bauman (2003)
中文：简《液态之爱》
`Philosophy & History of Love` · Late-modern frailty of bonds: dating as consumer choice and the fear of being tied.


**243. *Why Love Hurts*** — Eva Illouz (2012)
`Philosophy & History of Love` · A sociology of romantic suffering: choice, inequality, and the modern self that cannot bear ambivalence.


**244. *The End of Love*** — Eva Illouz (2019)
`Philosophy & History of Love` · Unloving as a social institution: ghosting, divorce, and the negative architecture of contemporary pairing.


**245. *Intimate Matters*** — John D'Emilio & Estelle B. Freedman (1988)
`Philosophy & History of Love` · A history of sexuality and intimate life in the United States — about how people actually paired.


**246. *Sister Outsider*** — Audre Lorde (1984)
`Philosophy & History of Love` · Includes “Uses of the Erotic”: the erotic as power and knowledge, foundational for later pleasure-and-justice writing.


**247. *The Argonauts*** — Maggie Nelson (2015)
中文：简《阿尔戈英雄》
`Philosophy & History of Love` · A hybrid memoir-theory of queer family-making, desire, and recognition; widely taught, not a niche pamphlet.


**248. *Big Friendship*** — Aminatou Sow & Ann Friedman (2020)
`Philosophy & History of Love` · A memoir-argument that a chosen friend can be a primary attachment — and can enter crisis like a marriage.


**249. *De Amicitia (On Friendship)*** — Cicero (44 BCE)
中文：《论友谊》／《論友誼》
`Philosophy & History of Love` · The Latin essay that taught Europe to treat friendship as a moral and civic intimacy, not a pastime.


**250. *On Friendship*** — Michel de Montaigne (1580)
中文：《论友谊》／《論友誼》
`Philosophy & History of Love` · “Because it was he, because it was I”: the early-modern statement of friendship as a unique, non-substitutable bond.

## What was left out (on purpose)

- **The rest of Luminary’s catalog.** *The Myth of Sisyphus*, *Four Thousand Weeks*, *Atomic Habits*, *The Psychology of Money*, *Meditations*, *Essentialism*, *Man’s Search for Meaning*, *Thinking, Fast and Slow*, *Tao Te Ching*, *Deep Work*, *When Breath Becomes Air*, *Never Split the Difference*, *Why We Sleep*, and *The Body Keeps the Score* are not relationship books and are not here. Only *The Courage to Be Disliked* stays.
- **General self-help and archetype books** that readers often file next to intimacy (*Daring Greatly*, *The Gifts of Imperfection*, *Iron John*, *Women Who Run with the Wolves*, *The Road Less Traveled*, *The Prophet*, *I’m OK — You’re OK*).
- **Workplace communication** (*Crucial Conversations*, *Fierce Conversations*, *Thanks for the Feedback*).
- **Pickup, “high-value,” and manipulation manuals.** They are about winning a market, not practicing intimacy.
- **Workbook-only or retitled editions** of a book already listed.
- **Parenting technique books** that do not turn back toward adult pairing.
- **Unattested or single-post “hidden gem” titles.**
- **Novels**, with one marked exception (*The Course of Love*).
- **Generic listening / friendship-as-lifestyle journalism** that is not an intimacy text.

## Uncertainty

A few popular Chinese titles (*为何家会伤人*, *巨婴国*) have later revised printings; the year given is the first widely cited edition. *More Than Two* is historically central to English-language polyamory and later shadowed by public allegations about a co-author — keep the entry, do not treat the book as a moral mascot. Evolutionary-psychology titles (Buss, *Sex at Dawn*) are arguments, not settled fact. Chapman, Gray, and Harley are folk psychology with enormous distribution. Chinese titles are standard publishing names, not new translations invented for this file; where 简 and 繁 editions disagree, both are listed.
