# Clay Sanctuary — five more rooms

Same mix as ranked direction 2: **Japandi × Wabi Sabi**. Same product: a relationship and intimacy editorial reader (daily read, library, calendar, bilingual English/Chinese). Not a dating app, not Blinkist, not Luminary’s Swiss poster.

The original still — three pale ceramic plates on oat plaster — is the *library-as-plates* cut. These five keep the mix and change the **room**: material, light, and which surface of the app you are looking at. None should be mistaken for the original.

## 1. Ink Desk

**Mix:** Japandi × Wabi Sabi, Japanese cut (ink, washi, one stroke).

**Why this room.** Intimacy writing is closer to a letter on a desk than to a ceramic catalog. This is the most *literary* Clay Sanctuary: a scholar’s table, wet ink, almost nothing else. English and Chinese sit as two columns of the same brush, not title-plus-subtitle.

**Palette / type / layout**
- Palette: washi `#F3EEE6`, diluted sumi `#4A4642`, one true-ink mark `#1C1A18`, a hint of residual cinnabar only in a date mark.
- Type: humanist sans for chrome; a Song / regular-script face for the essay. No display serif drama.
- Layout: one vertical ink stroke or wash as the only graphic, left or top. Title low. Huge empty paper. No tiles, no plates.

**Avoid.** Calligraphy-school ornament; red-stamp cliché overload; the original three-plate library; cream Light Academia.

**Still:** `media/clay-variation-01.png`

## 2. Pale Pine

**Mix:** Japandi × Wabi Sabi, Scandinavian cut (winter wood, cool light).

**Why this room.** The original is warm oat. This is the cold twin: a northern window, pale pine, a month you tend. Calendar is the right surface — days as knots in wood, not a habit heatmap. Quiet enough for two people to share a date without it looking like a couples tracker.

**Palette / type / layout**
- Palette: bleached pine `#E7E0D4`, cool ash `#B8B3AB`, winter white `#F6F4F0`, slate ink `#3A3A38`, a single moss knot `#6F7A68`.
- Type: clean grotesque, small. Month name large, days tiny.
- Layout: a sparse month on vertical pine boards or a wood-grain field. Today is one darker knot. No ceramic plates.

**Avoid.** Ski-lodge plaid; hygge candles; IKEA catalog cheer; spa mint.

**Still:** `media/clay-variation-02.png`

## 3. Iron Glaze

**Mix:** Japandi × Wabi Sabi, kiln-dark cut (tenmoku, iron spot, fired earth).

**Why this room.** If the original plates were bisque, these are fired. A heavier library — dark brown-black glaze, iron freckles, titles in warm gray. This is Clay Sanctuary’s night mode without borrowing Tenebrism or Candle Study’s spotlight. Private, grounded, a shelf after the lights go low.

**Palette / type / layout**
- Palette: iron black `#2A211C`, tenmoku brown `#5C3D2E`, ash glaze `#8A8076`, warm type `#E6DCCF`.
- Type: same humanist sans, lighter weight on dark clay.
- Layout: two or three irregular dark tiles, stacked like bowls, lots of unglazed void. Not the pale three-plate stack.

**Avoid.** Gothic black; gold rims; industrial Brutalism; neon.

**Still:** `media/clay-variation-03.png`

## 4. Tea Room

**Mix:** Japandi × Wabi Sabi, chashitsu cut (tatami, one bowl, dusk).

**Why this room.** The metaphor the mix already promised: a tea room for two, or for one. Evening without cinema. One celadon bowl is presence, not a streak. The essay arrives like the first pour — title set low, most of the field empty.

**Palette / type / layout**
- Palette: tatami `#C4B48A`, dusk plaster `#D9D0C2`, celadon `#7F9C8E`, charcoal `#2C2A28` used once.
- Type: small sans chrome; one line of Song for the Chinese title, equal to English.
- Layout: tatami texture as ground. One small bowl bottom-left or bottom-right. Title and a short paragraph in the remaining air. No plates, no wood calendar, no ink stroke.

**Avoid.** Bamboo-and-stones spa stock; matcha branding; geisha kitsch; full tatami-pattern wallpaper behind long text.

**Still:** `media/clay-variation-04.png`

## 5. Two Cups

**Mix:** Japandi × Wabi Sabi, domestic cut (oak table, morning, two uneven cups).

**Why this room.** This is the most *relationship-specific* room without becoming a dating product. Two cups, slightly different, on a worn table. The daily essay occupies the open grain. Intimacy as breakfast, not as a profile. Still no faces, no hands, no hearts.

**Palette / type / layout**
- Palette: oak `#C4A06A`–`#8B5E34`, milk glaze `#F2EBE0`, clay cup `#B08968`, celadon cup `#8AA392`, morning side-light.
- Type: quiet sans on the wood; short title only.
- Layout: tabletop fills the screen. Two cups at the edge, imperfect, not centered as a logo. Essay in the cleared space. Nav carved lightly into the wood.

**Avoid.** Couple photography; steam-heart latte art; farmhouse gingham; restaurant-menu layout.

**Still:** `media/clay-variation-05.png`

## How to choose among them

- **Ink Desk** if the product is a writer’s sanctuary (essay-first).
- **Pale Pine** if the calendar / shared-day ritual is the hero.
- **Iron Glaze** if you want Clay Sanctuary at night without leaving the mix.
- **Tea Room** if the brand metaphor is the room itself.
- **Two Cups** if the brand metaphor is *two people, one table*.

The original three-plate library remains a sixth option — the most catalog-like, and the one you already liked. A full system can keep one material language (unglazed earth, empty air, humanist type) and switch rooms by surface: Ink Desk for Read, plates or Iron Glaze for Library, Pale Pine for Calendar, Two Cups for Today’s open state.
